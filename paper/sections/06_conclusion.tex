\section{Conclusion}

The research currently establishes a working, reproducible empirical foundation for studying regime-dependent stock-return predictability. The pipeline pulls real CRSP monthly data through WRDS, constructs predictors and next-month targets using explicit calendar timing, labels volatility regimes from an expanding historical VIX median, estimates Elastic Net and XGBoost under forward-looking restrictions, and stores genuine out-of-sample predictions and evaluation artifacts.

The first preliminary real-data validation examines AAPL over 83 out-of-sample months from January 2019 through November 2025 using a reduced large-cap cross-section and the partial size-and-momentum predictor set. Neither Elastic Net nor XGBoost outperforms the stock-specific expanding historical-mean benchmark. XGBoost nevertheless produces slightly lower forecast errors and a less negative OOS $R^2$ than Elastic Net overall and in both volatility regimes. Both models are comparatively closer to the benchmark in HIGH-VIX months than in LOW-VIX months, although the evidence is not sufficient to establish regime-dependent predictive power.

These results should be interpreted as validation of the research design rather than as the final empirical conclusion. The next stage evaluates whether the models contain economically meaningful cross-sectional ranking information through predicted-return decile portfolios. Subsequent milestones add book-to-market through Compustat/CCM, expand to the complete CRSP production universe, finalize delisting-return treatment, evaluate turnover and transaction costs, and add formal inference and robustness analysis. The central research question therefore remains open while the empirical implementation progresses from software and single-stock validation to the full cross-sectional experiment.
