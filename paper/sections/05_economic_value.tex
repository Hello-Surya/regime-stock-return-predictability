\section{Economic Value}

The preliminary economic-value analysis sorts stocks on out-of-sample predicted next-month returns within each formation month and model. D1 contains the lowest predicted returns and D10 the highest, and the long--short portfolio is $D10-D1$. Results are computed separately using equal weights and lagged-market-equity value weights. The current specification uses only size and momentum predictors; book-to-market remains deferred until the Compustat/CCM stage.

\subsection{Cross-sectional sort validity}

A model-month is eligible for portfolio formation only when the prediction cross-section contains sufficient forecast dispersion to support ten portfolios. This restriction is economically important in the current sample. The tuned Elastic Net produces one identical forecast for every eligible stock in every one of the 83 OOS months. All 83 Elastic Net month-model groups are therefore excluded from decile formation, and no Elastic Net portfolio-return result is reported. Deterministic security-identifier tie-breaking is not used to manufacture deciles from an intercept-only forecast.

XGBoost produces non-degenerate prediction cross-sections in all 83 OOS months. Monthly eligible-stock counts range from 225 to 251, and every accepted month contains complete D1 through D10 portfolios. There are 42 HIGH-VIX formation months and 41 LOW-VIX formation months.

The equal-weighted XGBoost spread is economically close to zero overall: the mean monthly $D10-D1$ return is approximately -0.017\%. The HIGH-VIX equal-weighted spread is -0.151\% per month and the LOW-VIX spread is 0.119\% per month. These magnitudes provide little evidence of a robust equal-weighted return spread in the current preliminary specification.

The value-weighted results differ substantially. The overall value-weighted $D10-D1$ spread averages 0.708\% per month, corresponding to an annualized arithmetic mean of approximately 8.49\% and a descriptive Sharpe ratio of 0.42. This result is concentrated in LOW-VIX months: the LOW-VIX value-weighted spread averages 1.432\% per month, while the HIGH-VIX value-weighted spread is essentially zero. The LOW-VIX descriptive annualized arithmetic mean is approximately 17.18\%, with a descriptive Sharpe ratio of 1.09.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The value-weighted LOW-VIX spread is economically notable, but it should not be interpreted as broad cross-sectional ranking success. XGBoost's average monthly Spearman rank correlation between predicted and realized returns is slightly negative overall (-0.0101) and remains slightly negative in both regimes. Consequently, the positive LOW-VIX value-weighted spread appears to be concentrated in economically large stocks or in the tails of the predicted-return distribution rather than reflecting a uniformly monotonic ranking relation across the full cross-section.

The preliminary regime pattern also does not support a simple claim that predictability is stronger during HIGH-VIX periods. Under value weighting, the observed long--short spread is concentrated in LOW-VIX months; under equal weighting, both regime-specific spreads are small. This contrast is itself informative for the research question and motivates further analysis with the complete predictor set, formal inference, and additional portfolio diagnostics.

These results remain descriptive. No Newey--West/HAC significance tests, turnover adjustment, or transaction costs are incorporated in this table. The frozen transaction-cost robustness specification of 0 and 50 basis points one way, together with the full Compustat/CCM predictor panel and formal statistical inference, is reserved for subsequent stages.
