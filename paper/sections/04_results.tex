\section{Results}

\subsection{Preliminary single-stock out-of-sample validation}

Table~\ref{tab:aapl-preliminary} reports the first real-data out-of-sample validation for AAPL. The sample contains 83 prediction months from January 2019 through November 2025. These results are generated from models trained on the historical reduced cross-section; they are not models estimated on AAPL alone.

Neither model produces positive out-of-sample $R^2$ relative to the expanding historical-mean benchmark. Overall $R^2_{OOS}$ is -0.0274 for Elastic Net and -0.0162 for XGBoost. Thus, in this preliminary AAPL validation, the historical-mean benchmark has lower squared forecast error than either machine-learning model.

XGBoost provides a small but consistent improvement over Elastic Net. Its overall RMSE is 0.08052 compared with 0.08096 for Elastic Net, and its MAE is 0.06833 compared with 0.06873. XGBoost also has the less negative benchmark-relative OOS $R^2$ in both volatility regimes. These differences are modest and are not interpreted as evidence that nonlinearities are economically important at this stage.

\subsection{Performance across volatility regimes}

In HIGH-VIX months, Elastic Net and XGBoost produce OOS $R^2$ values of -0.0132 and -0.0067, respectively. In LOW-VIX months, the corresponding values are -0.0601 and -0.0384. Both models therefore perform comparatively closer to the historical-mean benchmark during high-volatility months than during low-volatility months.

This pattern is consistent with the research question but does not yet establish stronger return predictability in high-volatility regimes. All four regime-specific OOS $R^2$ values remain below zero.

\subsection{Cross-sectional ranking diagnostics}

The cross-sectional evaluation contains 83 OOS formation months from January 2019 through November 2025. Monthly eligible-stock counts range from 225 to 251, with a median of 240. The evaluation preserves the fixed top-300 research universe, applies the feature-month price screen, and uses only information observable by each formation date.

The tuned Elastic Net specification does not generate usable cross-sectional rankings. In every one of the 83 OOS months, all eligible stocks receive the same Elastic Net forecast. The portfolio code therefore classifies all 83 Elastic Net month-model groups as non-sortable because they contain fewer than ten distinct predictions. No Elastic Net decile or long--short result is reported. This is interpreted as a substantive null result for the current two-predictor regularized linear specification, not as a software failure.

XGBoost retains genuine forecast dispersion in all 83 OOS months and therefore supports cross-sectional ranking diagnostics. Its mean monthly Spearman correlation between predicted and realized next-month returns is -0.0101 overall, with a median of -0.0165 and a monthly standard deviation of 0.1049. Mean Spearman correlation is -0.0115 in HIGH-VIX months and -0.0086 in LOW-VIX months. The correlation is positive in 43.4\% of all OOS months. These diagnostics do not indicate broad monotonic ranking ability in the current preliminary specification.

\subsection{Additional diagnostics}

The AAPL prediction-realization correlations are weak or negative. Overall correlations are -0.2381 for Elastic Net and -0.1133 for XGBoost. Relative to a zero-return forecast, both models obtain positive diagnostic OOS $R^2$ values: approximately 0.0616 for Elastic Net and 0.0717 for XGBoost overall.

The cross-sectional results clarify why single-stock forecast metrics and portfolio evidence must be interpreted separately. The tuned Elastic Net can produce time-varying forecasts for a selected stock while still collapsing to an intercept-only forecast within each monthly cross-section. XGBoost, in contrast, produces cross-sectional dispersion, but its average rank correlation remains slightly negative. The associated portfolio-return evidence is reported separately in the economic-value section.

\subsection{Interpretation and current scope}

The current empirical stage supports four limited conclusions. First, the WRDS-based forecasting pipeline generates genuine out-of-sample predictions under explicit timing restrictions. Second, XGBoost modestly outperforms Elastic Net on forecast-error measures in the preliminary AAPL validation. Third, the tuned Elastic Net has no usable cross-sectional ranking signal in the current size-and-momentum specification. Fourth, XGBoost produces valid cross-sectional forecast dispersion, but its average monthly Spearman rank correlation is slightly negative. Portfolio return spreads are therefore treated as descriptive economic-value evidence rather than as proof of broad ranking skill. Formal inference, transaction-cost robustness, and the full Compustat/CCM predictor set remain for later stages.
