\begin{abstract}
This research studies whether next-month U.S. common-stock return predictability varies across high- and low-volatility market regimes and whether nonlinear machine learning captures this variation differently from a regularized linear benchmark. The production design combines CRSP monthly returns with Compustat accounting information through CCM, using log market equity, book-to-market, and momentum 12--2 as predictors. Volatility states are defined from the VIX relative to its expanding historical median, and Elastic Net and XGBoost are estimated under a strictly time-respecting expanding-window design. The out-of-sample evaluation contains 893,486 stock-month predictions across 311 formation months from January 2000 through November 2025. Return-level OOS $R^2$ is approximately zero overall for both models. Cross-sectional ranking results are more informative: XGBoost's mean monthly Spearman information coefficient is 0.0187 in LOW-VIX months and is statistically positive under Newey--West/HAC inference ($p=0.013$), while the corresponding HIGH-VIX estimate is near zero. Equal-weighted XGBoost $D10-D1$ portfolios in LOW-VIX months earn 0.969\% per month gross and 0.748\% per month after a 50-basis-point one-way turnover cost specification; the net estimate has HAC $t=2.82$ and $p=0.0049$. The effect disappears under value weighting, indicating concentration away from the largest stocks. Direct LOW-minus-HIGH rank-IC and portfolio-return differences are positive but not conventionally statistically significant. The production evidence therefore supports a cautious conclusion: nonlinear predictive content is concentrated in low-volatility states, but the direct regime contrast remains estimated imprecisely.
\end{abstract}
